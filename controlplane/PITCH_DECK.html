Why today's guardrails do not solve it03

Five failures, and they are structural rather than incidental

1
One global threshold cannot serve bothSet it where the copilot is comfortable and decision support leaks. Set it where decision support is safe and the copilot drowns in false alarms.
2
Binary blocking destroys utilityA pass or fail filter has exactly one response to uncertainty, and it is the most expensive one available.
3
Post-hoc observability is lateThe signal arrives after the refund was issued. Retraction costs more than prevention.
4
Risks overlap and resist categoriesA fabricated detail about a named person is a hallucination and a privacy incident at once. Counting it once understates it.
5
No ground truth at request timeThe gaps that cause hallucination make verification hard. Systems that read "could not verify" as "verified fine" fail silently and confidently.

Every team that ships a guardrail finds problem 1, then hand-tunes the threshold forever. That tuning is the job we automate.

The mechanism04

Treat every model output as a proposal to adjudicate, not an artifact to observe

For each response we estimate the probability it is defective and what a defect costs in this specific workflow, then take the action that minimises expected loss.

L(a) = ρ(a) · P_eff · C_eff · ι + F(a) + (1 − P_eff) · U(a) residual risk left behind friction utility destroyed by a false alarm

P_eff

Defect probability, including risk carried from earlier turns in the conversation.

C_eff · ι

What a defect costs here, discounted by how reversible the action is.

F(a), U(a)

What the intervention costs to run, and what it destroys when the response was fine.

The action taken is the argmin over five graded options. Detection is an input to this function, not the product. That is the distinction the whole submission rests on.

A correction worth stating out loud

Our first model priced escalation and blocking at the same utility loss. That makes L(ESCALATE) − L(BLOCK) = 0.1·p·C·ι + 70, positive at every probability, so BLOCK dominates ESCALATE everywhere and the argmin can never route to a human. A seeded run of 3,011 decisions contained zero escalations. Review delays a response; a block destroys it. Pricing them alike removes the human tier entirely.

Two mechanisms the brief asks for by name09

Bias, checked from outside the model

Enterprises consume models through an API, so weights and activations are out of reach and every representation-level fairness method is unavailable. Both of our detectors work from outside.

"The applicant is a Muslim woman from a scheduled caste, so I recommend we decline the loan."

religion · gender · caste, each beside a decision term

Categories follow the Indian constitutional grounds, Articles 15 and 16, because the policies declare jurisdiction IN. Caste and religion matter here in a way a US or EU list would miss. Precision 0.71, so it can demand a human and can never silence the model alone.

Abstention, when nothing can be checked

With no source to verify against, the detector reports verifiable = false and the engine substitutes that workflow's prior rather than zero.

Identical input, no branching on workflow name. Not being able to check something is not the same as it being fine, and a system that conflates the two fails silently.

Prototype status14

What is built, what is scoped out, and what is missing

Stated plainly and on purpose. A reviewer who finds an unimplemented feature themselves discounts everything else in the submission.

Built and demonstrable

Decision engine and derived thresholds. Precision caps. Abstention. Multi-turn compounding. Agentic reachability. Real NLI grounding that names the failing sentence. Protected-attribute and counterfactual bias detection. Tamper-evident ledger with concurrency-safe appends. Human adjudication loop. Applied calibration with a held-out gate. OpenAI-compatible proxy. Seven demo screens, 221 tests.

Deliberately out of scope

Inline rewriting of responses. Representation-level detection, which API consumption puts out of reach and which is precisely why our bias detection is counterfactual. Streaming. Authentication and multi-tenancy. These are decisions, not oversights.

Not yet built

Fail-open and fail-closed behaviour is validated but not consumed. No per-detector timeouts. Policy routing fractions declared but unused as gates. Tier 2 cost accounting understates self-consistency. Tier 1 and 2 judgement detectors are dial-controlled simulations, so detector quality stays a known quantity in the threshold measurements.

Known limits of the entailment model are documented too: it cannot do arithmetic over dates, it mishandles negation, and it marks elaboration as unsupported. That is why grounding carries 0.82 precision and is never permitted to block on its own.

Why this wins15

Most teams will build a better classifier. We built the layer above it.

Detection is hard and crowded, and a marginally better hallucination detector does not change how an enterprise governs AI. ControlPlane treats detection as an input and answers the question above it:

Given an imperfect signal, what is the right thing to do about it, in this workflow, at this cost, with this quality of evidence?

That layer does not exist in the market, it is where the money actually is, and it keeps working as detectors improve.

The single strongest piece of evidence

Our system measured its own detector, found it 5.6 times overconfident, failed its own calibration gate, and refused to let itself enforce.
